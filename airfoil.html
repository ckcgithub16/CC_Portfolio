Design and Manufacturing of a Load-Bearing Airfoil

March 2023 – May 2023

Abstract

Our team of nine designed and manufactured a 25.5-inch load-bearing airfoil that supported 97 lbf applied at 23.5 inches from the secured end while minimizing deflection, weight, and cost. The design centered on an aluminum 7075-T651 I-beam (yield stress 73,000 psi) with circular lightening holes, supported by nylon bulkheads and a balsa wood skin.

The final structure cost $180.75 and weighed 18.3 oz. During testing it passed the 97 lbf load (3.75 in. deflection), then failed at 120 lbs when the beam web collapsed due to torsion. Manufacturing used a Haas VF-7/40 CNC mill, a ProtoMAX waterjet, Creality Ender 3 Pro and Formlabs SLS 3D printers, and a bandsaw.

Design

Two parallel beam design teams explored solid I-beams, truss I-beams, circular-hole I-beams, and double I-beams. A separate group designed the bulkheads. After comparison of FEA results and manufacturing feasibility, we selected the circular-hole I-beam for its favorable strength-to-weight ratio.

The bulkheads were designed in nylon with circular cutouts to reduce weight while maintaining structural integrity. The leading edge was formed from a thin aluminum sheet bent to the NACA profile.

Manufacturing

The I-beam was CNC milled on a Haas VF-7/40. Bulkheads were waterjet-cut from aluminum sheet on a ProtoMAX, while ribs were printed on an Ender 3 Pro (FDM) and critical structural parts on a Formlabs SLS printer. The balsa skin was cut on a bandsaw and sanded to shape.

Waterjetted Aluminum Bulkhead

Testing, Results, and Conclusion

The airfoil passed the 97 lbf design load with 3.75 inches of tip deflection. Loading continued until failure at 120 lbs, when the beam web collapsed due to torsion — a failure mode not fully captured in our planar FEA model.

Key lessons: torsional loads must be explicitly modeled in 3D FEA, and physical load paths in complex assemblies can differ from simplified beam-theory predictions. The cost and weight targets were both met, making this a successful structural design exercise.
